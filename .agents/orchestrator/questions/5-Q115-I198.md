## 1. NaN or infinity into a whole-number type, with `lossy`
**Decision:** (a) an error, or (b) Rust's numbers · **Recommended:** (a) · **Why:** your answer's last sentence lands there

---

Issue #198 (one conversion step wherever a value meets a number type it is not) builds `lossy`, the word that writes a conversion which may lose part of the value. Q113 asked what `i32 lossy (x)` does when `x` is NaN ("not a number") or infinity:

```
x := f64 1.0 / 0.0, print «{i32 lossy (x)}»    # x is infinity
```

Your answer began with "b", then weighed it, and ended: "maybe a actually is most consistent because a number is quite generic and there are cases where you actuallly cannot convert between two numbers even with lossy." I do not pick between your two words myself, so I ask once more.

- (a) **An error when the program runs:** "this float is not a number or is infinite, so no whole number stands for it". `lossy` loses part of a value, but a value with no whole number at all is refused, as a complex number would be.
- (b) **Rust's numbers:** NaN gives `0`, infinity `2147483647` (the largest `i32`), minus infinity the smallest `i32`.

Recommended: (a), because it is where your reasoning ended: `lossy` may change a value, but some numbers have no value in the other type at all.
